01 — Introduction

What are Transition Elements?

Main Transition Elements

Elements that fill the d-sublevel. Located in the middle of the periodic table between representative elements and noble gases. Found in groups 3B–2B (periods 4–7).

General config: ns¹⁻² , (n-1)d¹⁻¹⁰
Example (Fe): [Ar] 4s² 3d⁶

Inner Transition Elements

Elements that fill the f-sublevel. Placed below the main table in two separate rows.

4f → Lanthanides (Rare Earth, Ce–Lu)
5f → Actinides (Radioactive, Th–Lr)

True Definition of a Transition Element

An element that has at least one unpaired (single) electron in the d-sublevel — either in its elemental form or in one of its ionic states.

✗ Zn — d¹⁰ always paired → NOT a true transition element
✓ Cu — d⁹ in Cu⁺² → IS a transition element
✓ Sc — d¹ in elemental form → IS a transition element

d-Sublevel Structure

The d-sublevel has 5 orbitals, each holding 2 electrons — max 10 electrons total. Their spin motion creates magnetic fields.

Period Number Calculation

= Principal quantum number of last s sublevel
OR = n of last d + 1

[Ar] 4s² 3d⁵ → Period 4
[Xe] 6s² 4f¹⁴ 5d → Period 6 (5+1)

General Group Number

= e⁻ in last s + e⁻ in last d

[Ar] 4s² 3d⁵ → Group 2+5 = 7 (7B)
[Kr] 5s² 4d³ → Group 2+3 = 5 (5B)

Special Group (d¹–d⁵)

For d¹, d², d³, d⁵: Group = e⁻ in s + e⁻ in d + Symbol B

d⁶, d⁷, d⁸ → Group 8 (VIII)
d¹⁰ → Group 2B
d¹ with 1s → Group 1B

No. of Series Rule

Series number = n of d sublevel, NOT n of s.
Formula: n (period) = n (d) + 1

3d → 1st series → Period 4
4d → 2nd series → Period 5
5d → 3rd series → Period 6

🔗 Iron Oxide Chain — Oxidation & Reduction

Chemical Reactions of Iron

① Reaction with O₂ (dry air)
3Fe + 2O₂ —[500°C]→ Fe₃O₄
Fe→grey, Fe₃O₄→black (increase in mass)
★ NOT Fe₂O₃ — O₂ is oxidizing agent but NOT strong enough to reach max oxidation state (+6 or even +3) in this reaction.
② Reaction with Humidity (H₂O vapour)
3Fe + 4H₂O(v) —[500°C]→ Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂
★ NOT Fe₂O₃ — H₂ produced is a strong reducing agent that PREVENTS reaching maximum oxidation state.
★ Differentiate: dry O₂ → Fe₃O₄ only; humid air → Fe₃O₄ + H₂ (H₂ detected by pop sound with burning)
③ Reaction with Halogens (Cl₂, Br₂, I₂)
2Fe + 3Cl₂ —[Δ]→ 2FeCl₃
2Fe + 3Br₂ —[Δ]→ 2FeBr₃
2Fe + 3I₂ —[Δ]→ 2FeI₃
Halogenation power: Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂
Group 7A → elements further right = stronger oxidizing agent
④ Reaction with HCl (dilute or concentrated)
Fe + 2HCl —[dil. or conc.]→ FeCl₂ + H₂
Fe→grey (no change in mass), FeCl₂→green
★ NOT FeCl₃ — H₂ is strong reducing agent preventing max oxidation state. H₂ also converts any FeCl₃ formed back to FeCl₂.
⑤ Reaction with H₂SO₄ dilute
Fe + H₂SO₄(dil) → FeSO₄ + H₂↑
Fe→grey, FeSO₄→green
H₂ detected by pop sound with burning flame.
⑥ Reaction with H₂SO₄ concentrated
3Fe + 8H₂SO₄(conc) → FeSO₄ + Fe₂(SO₄)₃ + 4SO₂ + 8H₂O
Conc. H₂SO₄ is very strong oxidizing agent — oxidizes H₂ into H₂O, overcoming its reducing effect.
Fe→ FeSO₄ (Fe²⁺, green) THEN Fe₂(SO₄)₃ (Fe³⁺, yellow)
⑦ Reaction with Conc. HNO₃ (Passivity)
Fe + Conc. HNO₃ → FeO (passivity layer)
Before:After ratio = 1:~1
Conc. HNO₃ causes passivity — forms FeO protective layer that prevents further reaction.
Piece of iron put 2 days in conc. HNO₃ then in dil. HCl → delayed H₂ bubbles due to passivity.
Remove passivity: chemically (dil. HCl) or physically (abrasion)
⑧ Roasting of Ores
Limonite:
2Fe₂O₃·3H₂O —[Δ]→ 2Fe₂O₃ + 3H₂O
Siderite:
2FeCO₃ —[Δ]→ 2FeO + 2CO₂
2FeO + ½O₂ —[400-700°C]→ Fe₂O₃
FeC₂O₄ (oxalate):
2FeC₂O₄ + 3/2O₂ —[Δ]→ Fe₂O₃ + 4CO₂
All end up as hematite (Fe₂O₃) for extraction.

  • • Direct product from blast furnace

  • • Low melting point due to carbon
  • • Used to make cast iron & steel

06 — Physical Properties

Physical Properties

⚡
Ionization Potential
Energy absorbed by an element in gaseous state to lose one electron and form a +ve ion (endothermic, ΔH = +ve).

Rule: High stability = Low ionization energy
3rd I.P. > 2nd I.P. > 1st I.P.
Fe: [Ar]4s²3d⁶ → stability at d⁵ → high 3rd I.P.
Transition elements have MULTIPLE ionization states
⚛️
Atomic Radius
Half the distance between centres of 2 nuclei in a diatomic molecule (Heisenberg principle: cannot detect exact position + velocity of electron simultaneously).

Factors: Attraction forces (↑ protons → ↑ attraction → ↓ radius) and Repulsion forces (↑ e⁻ → ↑ repulsion → ↑ radius)
Largest atom in 1st series: Sc (21)
Smallest atom in 1st series: Ni (28)
Cr–Mn–Cu: relative stability in atomic radius
Fe = Co (approximately equal radius)
🧲
Magnetic Properties
Any charged particle revolving around its axis creates a magnetic field and attracts external magnets (spin motion).

Paramagnetic = has single (unpaired) electrons in d-sublevel → coloured solutions
Diamagnetic = no single electrons in d-sublevel → colourless
Diamagnetic in 1st series: Sc³⁺, Ti⁴⁺, V⁵⁺, Cr⁶⁺, Mn⁷⁺, Cu, Cu⁺¹, Zn, Zn²⁺
Group 8 (Fe, Co, Ni) & compounds = always paramagnetic & coloured
Zn = ONLY element all states are diamagnetic
⚗️
Metallic Properties
All transition elements have general metallic properties: shiny metallic lustre, good conductor of electricity & heat, tend to lose electrons forming +ve ions.
Density: all high & higher than other elements in periodic table. ↑ atomic number → ↑ density
M.P. & B.P.: VERY HIGH due to large number of valence electrons lost from 4s & 3d → strong metallic bonds
🎨
Coloured Solutions
Coloured solutions contain a transition element with single electrons in the d-sublevel. All paramagnetic = coloured. Exception: KMnO₄ (Mn⁷⁺ diamagnetic but violet by charge transfer).
Fe³⁺ → pale yellow (except Fe(SCN)₃ = blood red)
Fe²⁺, Cr³⁺, Ni²⁺ → green (except FeC₂O₄ = yellow)
Cu²⁺, V³⁺ → blue
KMnO₄ → violet (exception)
K₂Cr₂O₇ → orange (exception)
V₂O₅ → blue-green (exception)
🔬
Alloy Types
Alloy = mixture between metal and element(s). Must contain at least one metal (non-metal + non-metal is NOT an alloy).
① Substitution alloy: one metal distorts crystal lattice of another (e.g. Mn-Fe, Ni-Cr, Au-Cu)
② Interstitial alloy: small molecules fill intermolecular spaces (e.g. Fe-C in steel, X=1.17Å, C=0.02Å)
③ Intermetallic: chemical connections (e.g. Fe₃C cementite, Au₂Pb, Ni₃Al)

10 Rules for Calculating Oxidation Number

01
Oxidation number of any element in pure form = 0 (Na, Ar, H₂, O₂, Cl₂, Fe)
02
Group IA elements in compounds = +1 (Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs)
03
Group IIA elements in compounds = +2 (Mg, Ca, Ba, Sr)
04
Group IIIA elements in compounds = +3 (Al³⁺)
05
Fluorine in all compounds = −1 (most electronegative)
06
Halogens (Group VIIA) in compounds = −1 mostly (Cl⁻, Br⁻, I⁻)
07
Oxygen in compounds = −2 mostly (except peroxides = −1, OF₂ = +2)
08
Hydrogen in compounds = +1 mostly (except metal hydrides = −1)
09
Sum of all oxidation numbers in a compound = zero. Example: KMnO₄ → (+1)+Mn+4(−2)=0, Mn=+7
10
Sum of oxidation numbers in an atomic group = its charge. NH₄⁺: N+4H=+1; NO₃⁻: N+3O=−1
08 — Exam Preparation ★

Key Notes & Exam Tips

🧬
Anomalous Config Rule
Electron "jumps" from 4s → 3d to achieve half-filled (d⁵) or completely filled (d¹⁰) — both are extra-stable configurations.
Cr: [Ar] 4s¹ 3d⁵ (not 4s² 3d⁴)
Cu: [Ar] 4s¹ 3d¹⁰ (not 4s² 3d⁹)
Mo, Ag, Au follow same pattern
⚡
Electron Removal Order
Always lose electrons from outermost sublevel first — s before d (s is farther from nucleus).
Cu: [Ar] 4s¹ 3d¹⁰ (atomic)
Cu⁺¹: [Ar] 3d¹⁰ (s lost first)
Cu⁺²: [Ar] 3d⁹ (then d lost)
🔢
d⁴ & d⁹ Don't Exist in Elemental Form
Due to anomalous configs, d⁴ and d⁹ spontaneously convert to d⁵ or d¹⁰ in elemental form. But they CAN exist in ionic form!
Cr²⁺: [Ar] 3d⁴ ← ionic (exists)
Cu²⁺: [Ar] 3d⁹ ← ionic (exists)
Cr (elemental): always d⁵ ✓
📊
Iron Reaction Key Points
Fe reacts to form Fe₃O₄ (not Fe₂O₃) with both dry O₂ and humidity because O₂ is not strong enough and H₂ is a reducing agent preventing max oxidation.
Fe + dry O₂ → Fe₃O₄ only
Fe + H₂O(v) → Fe₃O₄ + H₂↑
Fe + HCl → FeCl₂ + H₂↑ (green)
H₂ = detected by pop sound + burn
🧲
Special Alloys to Remember
Key alloys frequently tested in exams — remember composition and uses.
Stainless Steel = Fe + Cr
Bronze = Cu + Sn (statues, medals)
Brass = Cu + Zn (door handles)
Nitinol = Ti + Ni (teeth corrections)
Steel = Fe + C + Ni + Si + Cr
Sc-Al = MIG jets, weapons
⚗️
Catalysts Summary
Transition metals are excellent catalysts due to multiple oxidation states and ability to form intermediate compounds.
V₂O₅ → SO₂→SO₃ (Contact, 450°C)
V₂O₅ → Benzoic acid (400°C)
Fe/Mo → Haber-Bosch NH₃
FeCl₃ → Halogenation benzene
MnO₂ → H₂O₂ decomposition
Ni → Hydrogenation veg. oils
ZnCl₂ → Alcohol reactions
CuCrO₄ → Reduce carboxylic acids
🎨
Colours of Solutions
Paramagnetic = coloured. Group 8 (Fe, Co, Ni) compounds always coloured. Exceptions exist for diamagnetic compounds due to charge transfer.

Iron Ores — Quick Comparison
Only Hematite is used for extraction because it has high Fe% (69.6%) and is easy to reduce. Others must be roasted first.
Hematite Fe₂O₃ → red, 69.6% ✓ USED
Limonite 2Fe₂O₃·3H₂O → yellow, 45%
Siderite FeCO₃ → grey, 48%
Magnetite Fe₃O₄ → black, 70% ✗ magnetic
