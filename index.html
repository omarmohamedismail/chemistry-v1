01 — Introduction

What are Transition Elements?

Main Transition Elements

Fill the d-sublevel. Located in the middle of the periodic table between representative elements and noble gases (Groups 3B–2B).

General: ns¹⁻² , (n-1)d¹⁻¹⁰
Iron example: [Ar] 4s² 3d⁶
Expanded: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d⁶ 4s²

Inner Transition Elements

Fill the f-sublevel. Placed below the main table in two rows.

4f → Lanthanides (Rare Earth, Ce–Lu)
5f → Actinides (Radioactive, Th–Lr)

True Definition of Transition Element

Has at least one unpaired electron in d-sublevel — in elemental form OR one ionic state.

✗ Zn — d¹⁰ always paired → NOT true transition
✓ Cu — d⁹ in Cu²⁺ state → IS transition
✓ Sc — d¹ in elemental form → IS transition

d-Sublevel: 5 Orbitals, 10 Electrons

Each orbital holds 2 electrons. Unpaired electrons spin → magnetic field → paramagnetism → coloured compounds.

d¹⁰ COMPLETELY FILLED

Period Number

= n of last s sublevel, OR n of last d + 1

[Ar] 4s² 3d⁵ → Period 4
[Xe] 6s² 4f¹⁴ 5d → Period 6 (5+1)

Group Number

= electrons in last s + electrons in last d

[Ar] 4s² 3d⁵ → 2+5=7 (7B)
[Kr] 5s² 4d³ → 2+3=5 (5B)
d⁶, d⁷, d⁸ → Group 8 (VIII)

Physical Processes (decrease in mass):

  • Concentration: ONLY process to separate impurities as SOLIDS

🔥 Blast Furnace (الفرن العالي) — High Furnace

Zone ① — 300°C (Top)
3Fe₂O₃ + CO → 2Fe₃O₄ + CO₂ (partial reduction)
Zone ② — 500°C
Fe₃O₄ + CO → 3FeO + CO₂ (intermediate, not magnetite)
Zone ③ — >700°C (Final Reduction)
FeO + CO → Fe + CO₂
Total: Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂
Zone ④ — 900°C (Limestone/Slag)
CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ (quicklime)
CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃ (calcium silicate = SLAG)
Slag = filter for Fe impurities. Rich in P → used as fertilizer
Zone ⑤ — 1200–1500°C (Tuyeres)
C + O₂ → CO₂ (coke combustion)
CO₂ + C → 2CO (CO = reducing agent)
Hot air blown in through tuyeres (نافثات)
Products of Blast Furnace
• Pig Iron (حديد خام): ~4% C impurities
Poured directly in sand moulds (tanglets)
Used for cast iron & steel production
• Spongy Iron (حديد إسفنجي): least %C
Used for wroughting iron (حديد مطاوع)
Most malleable form of iron

🔵 Madrex Furnace (فرن مادريكس) — Medium Furnace

Uses Water Gas (CO + H₂) as reducing agent instead of pure CO from coke.

Source of Water Gas (Natural Gas):
2CH₄ + CO₂ + H₂O →[Δ]→ 3CO + 5H₂
Oxidizing agents (reduced) in this furnace:
Fe₂O₃, O₂, CO₂

Reducing agents (oxidized):
C, CO, CH₄, CO+H₂

No oxidation/reduction (spectators):
Ca²⁺, Si⁴⁺
Main Reduction Reaction:
2Fe₂O₃ + 3CO + 3H₂ →[>700°C]→
4Fe + 3CO₂ + 3H₂O
Advantages over Blast Furnace:
• More efficient — uses water gas
• Produces spongy iron directly
• Less energy waste
• Better control of carbon content
Products from both furnaces:
• Pig Iron (حديد خام) — ~4% C, poured in sand tanglets → used for cast iron production
• Spongy Iron (حديد إسفنجي) — least %C, used for wroughting iron (حديد مطاوع), most malleable

🔗 Iron Oxide Oxidation & Reduction Chain

Chemical Reactions of Iron

① With O₂ (dry air)
3Fe + 2O₂ —[500°C]→ Fe₃O₄
Fe→grey; Fe₃O₄→black; mass increases
★ NOT Fe₂O₃ — O₂ is not strong enough oxidizing agent to reach maximum oxidation state of iron
② With Humidity H₂O(v)
3Fe + 4H₂O(v) —[500°C]→ Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂
★ NOT Fe₂O₃ — H₂ produced is strong reducing agent PREVENTING reaching max oxidation state
Differentiate: dry O₂ → Fe₃O₄ only; humid air → Fe₃O₄ + H₂↑ (pop sound with burning)
③ With Cl₂ (Group 7A)
2Fe + 3Cl₂ —[Δ]→ 2FeCl₃ (yellow)
2Fe + 3Br₂ —[Δ]→ 2FeBr₃
2Fe + 3I₂ —[Δ]→ 2FeI₃
Fe→grey; FeCl₃→yellow; mass increases
Cl₂ IS strong enough to reach Fe³⁺
Oxidizing power: Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂ > O₂ > S
④ With S (Group 6A)
Fe + S —[Δ]→ FeS (black, solid)
Fe→grey; FeS→black; mass increases (no change in μ)
★ NOT Fe₂S₃ — S is oxidizing agent but NOT strong enough to reach max oxidation state
⑤ With HCl (dil. or conc.)
Fe + 2HCl —[dil. or conc.]→ FeCl₂ + H₂↑
Fe→grey; FeCl₂→green; no change in mass
★ NOT FeCl₃ — H₂ produced prevents reaching max state & converts any FeCl₃ back to FeCl₂
★ Cannot differentiate dil. HCl from conc. HCl — same products!
⑥ With H₂SO₄ dilute
Fe + H₂SO₄(dil) → FeSO₄ + H₂↑
FeSO₄→green; no change in mass
H₂ detected by pop sound with burning
Dil. H₂SO₄ not strong enough to reach max oxidation state
⑦ With H₂SO₄ concentrated
3Fe + 8H₂SO₄(conc) → FeSO₄ + Fe₂(SO₄)₃ + 4SO₂↑ + 8H₂O
Conc. H₂SO₄ oxidizes H₂ into H₂O (overcomes reducing effect)
Fe→FeSO₄ (green) THEN Fe₂(SO₄)₃ (yellow-green)
SO₂ detected: turns K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄ paper from orange→green
⑧ With HNO₃ dilute
Fe + 4HNO₃(dil) → Fe(NO₃)₃ + NO↑ + 2H₂O
Fe→grey; Fe(NO₃)₃→yellow; mass increases
Only dil. HNO₃ produces Fe³⁺ — strong enough to reach +3 oxidation state
⑨ With HNO₃ concentrated (Passivity)
Fe + HNO₃(conc) → FeO layer (passivity)
Forms large-sized, non-porous FeO layer — prevents further reaction (similar to Cr and Al passivity)
★ Iron in conc. HNO₃ for 2 days, then dil. HCl → delayed H₂ bubbles
Remove passivity: chemically (dil. HCl) or physically (abrasion/صنفرة)
⑩ Double Substitution → Fe(OH)₃
FeCl₃ + 3NH₄OH → Fe(OH)₃↓ + 3NH₄Cl
2Fe(OH)₃ →[>200°C]→ Fe₂O₃ + 3H₂O
Fe(OH)₃ = iron III hydroxide = reddish-brown (rusting/تصدأ)
Use any Fe³⁺ salt: FeCl₃, Fe₂(SO₄)₃, FeBr₃, Fe(NO₃)₃
Use any alkali: NaOH, KOH, NH₄OH
No colour change (Fe³⁺ stays Fe³⁺)
⑪ Iron Oxides with Acids
FeO + H₂SO₄(dil) → FeSO₄ + H₂O
Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂SO₄(conc) → FeSO₄+Fe₂(SO₄)₃+4H₂O
Fe₂O₃ + 3H₂SO₄(conc) → Fe₂(SO₄)₃ + 3H₂O
FeO — reacts with dilute acids
Fe₃O₄ — reacts ONLY with concentrated acids
Fe₂O₃ — reacts ONLY with concentrated acids
⑫ How to Obtain Specific Oxides
Fe→Fe₂O₃: 3Fe+2O₂→Fe₃O₄(500°C)
then 2Fe₃O₄+½O₂→3Fe₂O₃(230-300°C)
Fe→FeO: above, then Fe₃O₄+H₂→3FeO+H₂O
Fe₃O₄→Fe: 2Fe₃O₄+½O₂→3Fe₂O₃
then Fe₂O₃+3CO→2Fe+3CO₂(>700°C)
Oxidation/Reduction chain enables conversion between all iron oxides

Types of Iron Products

Pig Iron (حديد خام)

~96%Fe + 4%C + Si+Mn+P+S
  • Direct product from blast/madrex furnace
  • Very brittle, high carbon content
  • Poured directly in sand tanglets
  • Used to make cast iron & steel

Cast Iron (حديد زهر)

Fe + C(2–4%) + impurities
  • Made from pig iron with less C
  • Hard but brittle — cannot be forged
  • Engine blocks, pipes, cookware
  • Fe₃C (cementite) is intermetallic

Steel / Wrought Iron (الصلب)

Fe–C–Ni–Si–Cr (C < 2%)
  • Steps: ①Reduction ②Oxidation ③Add C
  • Strong, ductile, not brittle
  • Stainless = Fe+Cr (passivity)
  • Construction, tools, cars, bridges

06 — Properties

Physical Properties

⚡

Ionization Potential

Energy absorbed by gaseous element to lose one electron → +ve ion (endothermic, ΔH = +ve).

Rule: High stability = Low ionization energy
3rd I.P. > 2nd I.P. > 1st I.P.

Main TE = multiple ionization potential states
Will be at one valence state when stable
V: E₁→V⁺¹→E₂→V⁺²→E₃→V⁺³(stable d²)
V⁺³ more stable than V⁺⁴ (E₆ very high)
⚛️

Atomic Radius

Half the distance between nuclei in diatomic molecule (Heisenberg principle).

Factors: Attraction forces (↑ protons → ↑ attraction → ↓ radius) and Repulsion forces

Largest in 1st series: Sc (21)
Smallest in 1st series: Ni (28)
From Cr→Cu: attraction≈repulsion forces
Fe ≈ Co (approximately equal radius)
🧲

Magnetic Properties

Charged particles revolving → magnetic field.

Paramagnetic = single e⁻ in d-sublevel → coloured
Diamagnetic = no single e⁻ → colourless

Diamagnetic in 1st series:
Sc³⁺, Ti⁴⁺, V⁵⁺, Cr⁶⁺, Mn⁷⁺,
Cu, Cu⁺¹, Zn, Zn²⁺
Group 8 (Fe,Co,Ni): always paramagnetic
★ Zn = ONLY all-diamagnetic element
🔥

Melting Point, B.P. & Density

All transition elements have very high M.P. & B.P. due to many valence electrons → strong metallic bonds.

↑ valence e⁻ → ↑ electron cloud density
→ ↑ metallic bond strength
→ ↑ hardness → ↑ M.P. & B.P.
Density: higher than other elements
↑ atomic number → ↑ density

Rules for Coloured Solutions

Coloured = single electrons in d-sublevel (paramagnetic)
Colourless = no single electrons in d-sublevel, OR no transition element at all
Exceptions (diamagnetic but coloured): KMnO₄ (violet), K₂Cr₂O₇ (orange), V₂O₅ (blue-green) — due to charge transfer phenomenon
All paramagnetic substances = coloured solutions
All Group 8 compound solutions = always coloured

10 Rules for Oxidation Numbers

01
Pure element = 0 (Na, Ar, H₂, O₂, Cl₂, Fe)
02
Group IA in compounds = +1 (Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs)
03
Group IIA in compounds = +2 (Mg, Ca, Ba, Sr)
04
Group IIIA in compounds = +3 (Al³⁺)
05
Fluorine in all compounds = −1 (most electronegative)
06
Halogens (7A) = −1 mostly (Cl⁻, Br⁻, I⁻)
07
Oxygen = −2 mostly (peroxides = −1, OF₂ = +2)
08
Hydrogen = +1 mostly (metal hydrides = −1)
09
Sum in neutral compound = 0. KMnO₄: (+1)+Mn+4(−2)=0 → Mn=+7
10
Sum in atomic group = its charge. NH₄⁺: N+4H=+1; CO₃²⁻: C+3O=−2

08 — Exam Prep ★

Key Notes & Exam Tips

🧬
Anomalous Config Rule
Electron "jumps" 4s→3d to achieve half-filled (d⁵) or fully filled (d¹⁰). d⁴ and d⁹ only exist in ionic form!
Cr: [Ar] 4s¹ 3d⁵ (not 4s² 3d⁴)
Cu: [Ar] 4s¹ 3d¹⁰ (not 4s² 3d⁹)
Ag, Mo, Au follow same pattern
🔥
Iron Reactions — WHAT & WHY
Fe always forms Fe₃O₄ (not Fe₂O₃) with O₂ and H₂O because O₂ is not strong enough & H₂ produced reduces Fe back.
Fe+O₂ → Fe₃O₄ (NOT Fe₂O₃)
Fe+H₂O(v) → Fe₃O₄+H₂↑
Fe+HCl → FeCl₂+H₂↑ (NOT FeCl₃)
Fe+HNO₃(dil) → Fe³⁺ (only dil!)
Fe+HNO₃(conc) → passivity
🔥
Blast Furnace Zones (5 zones)
5 temperature zones, each with specific reactions. CO is the reducing agent produced from coke combustion.
①300°C: Fe₂O₃→Fe₃O₄
②500°C: Fe₃O₄→FeO
③>700°C: FeO→Fe
④900°C: CaCO₃→CaO→slag
⑤1200-1500°C: C+O₂→CO₂→2CO
🔵
Madrex vs Blast Furnace
Blast: uses CO from coke as reducing agent
Madrex: uses water gas (CO+H₂) from natural gas — more efficient
Both produce pig iron & spongy iron
Pig iron: ~4% C → cast iron & steel
Spongy iron: least %C → wrought iron
Madrex: 2Fe₂O₃+3CO+3H₂→4Fe+...
🔬
Iron Ores — Quick Ref
Only Hematite used for extraction. Others need roasting to convert to hematite first.
Fe₂O₃ (red) 69.6% ✓ ONLY USED
2Fe₂O₃·3H₂O (yellow) 45%
FeCO₃ (grey) 48%
Fe₃O₄ (black) 70% ✗ magnetic
⚗️
Catalysts Summary
Transition metals = excellent catalysts due to variable oxidation states forming intermediate compounds.
V₂O₅ → H₂SO₄ (450°C, contact)
V₂O₅ → benzoic acid (400°C)
Fe/Mo → Haber-Bosch NH₃
FeCl₃ → halogenation benzene
MnO₂ → decompose H₂O₂
Ni → hydrogenation oils
ZnCl₂ → alcohol reactions
CuCrO₄ → reduce carboxylic acids
🎨
Colours Summary
Paramagnetic = coloured. Group 8 always coloured. Exceptions = charge transfer phenomenon.
Fe²⁺=green · Fe³⁺=pale yellow
Fe(SCN)₃=blood red ★exception
FeC₂O₄=yellow ★exception
KMnO₄=violet ★exception (diamagnetic)
K₂Cr₂O₇=orange ★exception
Zn=only all-diamagnetic element
🧲
Alloys to Remember
Three types: substitution (one distorts other's lattice), interstitial (small in spaces), intermetallic (chemical bonds).
Stainless = Fe+Cr (passivity)
Bronze = Cu+Sn (statues/medals)
Brass = Cu+Zn (door handles)
Nitinol = Ti+Ni (teeth corrections)
Steel = Fe+C+Ni+Si+Cr
Ferromanganese = Fe+Mn (railway)
Sc-Al = MIG jets, weapons
